## Outcome

Produce an approved, bounded, and testable requirements baseline. Functional behavior is expressed as BDD scenarios. Non-functional expectations are expressed as measurable limits with verification methods. The output must be sufficient to plan TDD implementation increments and identify critical E2E journeys.

## Execution prompt

Use the following prompt to execute this task with a human analyst or an AI assistant:

```text
Act as a requirements analyst working on WBS-002.

Goal:
Transform the supplied project idea and stakeholder answers into an approved,
testable requirements baseline in doc/task-desc.md.

Method:
1. Ask only questions needed to resolve product scope, actors, workflows, data,
   integrations, constraints, risks, and measurable success.
2. Never invent domain facts. Record missing facts as OQ-### open questions.
3. Assign every requirement a stable REQ-### identifier and Must/Should/Could/
   Won't-now priority.
4. Separate functional requirements from non-functional requirements.
5. For every functional Must requirement, write at least one BDD scenario using
   Given/When/Then. Include negative, authorization, and recovery scenarios when
   applicable.
6. For every applicable non-functional requirement, define a numeric or otherwise
   objectively verifiable limit, test conditions, and verification method.
7. Identify which behaviors will be driven by unit/component TDD and which critical
   journeys require E2E verification. Do not write production code in WBS-002.
8. Detect contradictions, vague words, hidden dependencies, and unowned decisions.
9. Update traceability so REQ-### items map to scenarios, future tests, ADR topics,
   and WBS work.
10. Stop at the task limits below and request approval from the named approver.

Required output:
- Problem statement and measurable outcome
- Stakeholders and actors
- In-scope and out-of-scope behavior
- Functional requirements with BDD scenarios
- Non-functional requirements with measurable limits
- Data and integration inventory
- TDD and E2E verification map
```

## Inputs

- Initial product or project idea
- Sponsor, product owner, or delegated decision maker
- Known users and operational stakeholders
- Existing processes, systems, interfaces, and data sources
- Known policy, budget, schedule, platform, security, and compliance constraints

If an input is unavailable, create an owned open question. Do not convert an unknown into a requirement.

## Functional scope limits

WBS-002 may define and prioritize behavior, but it must not design or implement the solution.

### In scope

- Actor goals and permissions
- Trigger, precondition, normal flow, alternative flow, and error/recovery behavior
- Business rules, calculations, state changes, validations, and notifications
- Input/output behavior and external-system interactions
- Acceptance examples written as BDD scenarios
- Explicit first-release functional boundary

### Out of scope

- Production code, test automation, database schema, API implementation, or UI implementation
- Selecting frameworks, cloud vendors, databases, or architecture styles without an approved constraint
- Detailed estimates before Must requirements and dependencies are stable
- Features without a named stakeholder, rationale, and acceptance evidence
- Expanding the first release with Should/Could items unless the approver changes priority

### Functional requirement limit

Each functional requirement must contain exactly one primary capability and must be independently testable. Split requirements joined by “and” when either behavior can succeed or fail independently. Every Must requirement needs:

- at least one successful BDD scenario;
- at least one negative or boundary scenario when failure is possible;
- authorization and recovery scenarios when applicable;
- explicit input, observable output, and side effects;
- traceability to an actor and measurable outcome.

## Non-functional scope limits

Non-functional requirements constrain how well the system must operate. WBS-002 records required outcomes and test conditions, not implementation mechanisms.

### In scope

- Performance and capacity
- Availability, reliability, resilience, backup, and recovery
- Security, privacy, auditability, and compliance
- Accessibility and supported user environments
- Observability, supportability, maintainability, and operability
- Data retention, deletion, residency, and integrity
- Deployment, compatibility, and interoperability constraints

### Out of scope

- Unmeasurable terms such as “fast,” “secure,” “scalable,” or “user-friendly” without a metric
- Invented numeric targets not supplied or approved by an accountable stakeholder
- Tool or vendor choices presented as quality requirements
- Universal quality goals unrelated to the identified users, risks, or operating context

### Non-functional requirement limit

Each applicable non-functional requirement must specify:

- the measured property and unit;
- the threshold or range;
- workload and environmental conditions;
- measurement window and percentile where relevant;
- verification method and test environment;
- accountable owner and source;
- failure response or exception process.

When a threshold is unknown, use `TBD` and create an `OQ-###`; never fabricate a number.

## BDD specification format

Use one feature per user or business capability and tag it with requirement IDs.

```gherkin
@REQ-### @must
Feature: Short capability name
  As a <role>
  I want <capability>
  So that <measurable benefit>

  Background:
    Given <shared verified context>

  Scenario: Successful outcome
    Given <initial state>
    When <actor performs one meaningful action>
    Then <observable outcome>
    And <required side effect>

  Scenario: Rejected boundary or failure
    Given <boundary or invalid state>
    When <actor performs the action>
    Then <safe observable rejection>
    And <state remains consistent>
```

Scenarios describe externally observable behavior. They must not mention classes, private functions, database tables, or other implementation details unless those details are fixed external constraints.

## TDD handoff rules

WBS-002 does not implement tests. It prepares the implementation handoff:

1. Map each business rule, validation, calculation, and state transition to a future unit or component test area.
2. Mark interfaces to external systems for contract-test planning.
3. Identify boundary values and error cases from the BDD examples.
4. Require future implementation tasks to follow red → green → refactor.
5. Keep unit/component tests deterministic, isolated, and fast; do not duplicate full E2E flows at the unit layer.

## E2E handoff rules

Select the smallest set of journeys that proves the first release works across its actual boundaries. An E2E candidate should cover one or more of:

- the primary value-producing journey;
- a critical authorization or privacy boundary;
- a high-risk external integration;
- a destructive or irreversible action;
- a critical failure and recovery path.

Do not designate every BDD scenario as E2E. Record which scenarios are better verified by unit, component, contract, or E2E tests to keep the suite reliable and economical.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A product owner and approver are named.
- [ ] Scope, exclusions, actors, and measurable success are approved.
- [ ] Functional and non-functional requirements are separated and uniquely identified.
- [ ] Every Must functional requirement satisfies the functional requirement limit.
- [ ] Every applicable non-functional requirement satisfies the non-functional requirement limit.
- [ ] BDD scenarios describe normal and relevant exceptional behavior without implementation details.
- [ ] A verification matrix distinguishes TDD, contract, E2E, and non-functional coverage.
- [ ] Critical E2E journeys are few, explicit, and tied to business or operational risk.
- [ ] Contradictions and missing facts are recorded as owned open questions.
- [ ] No architecture, vendor, or numeric quality target has been invented.
- [ ] The approver has accepted the baseline or all objections are recorded.

## Stop conditions

Stop and mark WBS-002 blocked if any of these remains true:

- no accountable approver is available;
- the problem or target user cannot be stated;
- first-release Must scope cannot be distinguished from later scope;
- a P0 security, privacy, legal, data-ownership, or integration question is unresolved;
- success cannot be observed or measured;
- conflicting Must requirements lack a decision owner.
